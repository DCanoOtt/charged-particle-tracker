11.1 Transport. Condensed-history Monte Carlo in cm and MeV. Every ion advances in steps of at most 10 % of its remaining range (the first step in each layer has a random length so that step boundaries do not imprint structure on the spectra). In a step the mean energy loss comes from the electronic stopping power (the tabulated data set chosen under Physics, or the built-in Bethe-based model with shell, Barkas and Bloch corrections and a low-energy part) plus the nuclear stopping (ZBL), with Gaussian straggling; the direction changes by multiple scattering plus single hard Rutherford collisions; energy transfers above Tcut create tracked δ-electrons; a nuclear reaction removes the projectile with the probability n σ ℓ of the step (ℓ = step length, n the atom density).
