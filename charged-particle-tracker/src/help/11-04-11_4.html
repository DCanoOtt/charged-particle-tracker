11.4 Energy and angular distributions of the secondaries. The 1000 energy bins of the flux are grouped in blocks of eight; each block gives a weight W = Σ n σ L y and a mean projectile energy E. W reactions are distributed over the outgoing energy Eo and the lab angle θ with the MF 6 data of each product (the angle-integrated spectrum is also computed by numerical angular integration):

  • LAW 1 (continuum): f(E′,μ) = f0(E′)·g(μ), with f0 the tabulated energy distribution (linearly interpolated in E′ and between the tabulated incident energies) and g(μ) either a Legendre series Σ (2l+1)/2 blPl(μ) or the Kalbach-Mann form g = a/(2 sinh a)·[cosh aμ + r sinh aμ]. If the file gives only r (ENDF NA = 1, as in TENDL), the slope a is calculated from the Kalbach systematics for an incident α: a = 0.04 X1 + 1.8·10−6 X13, X1 = min(ea,130)·eb/ea, ea = Ecm + Sa, eb = E′ + Sb; the separation energies Sa and Sb = Sa − QM come from liquid-drop binding energies (an estimate: the angular shape is approximate).
  • LAW 2 (two-body, discrete level): the CM energy of the ejectile is fixed by the kinematics, E′ = AR/(AR+Ap)·(Ecm + Q), with Ecm = E·At/(At+Ai) (Ai = projectile mass), AR = At+Ai−Ap and Q the level Q-value; the angular distribution is Legendre or tabulated.
  • LAW 6 (N-body phase space): isotropic in the CM with f(E′) ∝ √E′ (E′max − E′)3N/2−4. Other laws are skipped (reported on the page).

Projectile-dependent quantities. Ai is the projectile mass in neutron masses (atomic masses of H-1, H-2, H-3, He-3, He-4 divided by the neutron mass); in the Kalbach-Mann slope the separation energy of the projectile from the compound nucleus uses its binding energy (0, 2.2246, 8.4818, 7.7181, 28.2957 MeV for p, d, t, ³He, α) and Ma = 1 for p, d, t, ³He and 0 for α (the ENDF-6 manual gives Ma = 1, 1, 1, 0 for n, p, d, α; for t and ³He, which Kalbach did not specify, 1 is used — the term is small). The level series of the projectile’s own species (MT 600 for p, 650 d, 700 t, 750 ³He, 800 α) starts with the ground state, which is elastic scattering and is not part of the non-elastic cross section: that one channel is left out of the sum of channels.

Two-body kinematics (default). The tabulated E′ and angle are taken as centre-of-mass quantities. With the CM velocity, an ejectile of mass Ap has Ec = ApAiE/(Ai+At)² (the energy it would have if it moved with the CM) and the lab energy is E = E′ + Ec + 2√(E′Ec)·cosθ′. To obtain the distribution at a fixed lab angle θ, the relation is inverted: (√E cosθ − √Ec, √E sinθ) gives E′ and cosθ′, and flab(E,Ω) = fcm(E′,Ω′)·√(E/E′) (phase-space invariance, d³v is conserved), with f per steradian = f(E′,μ′)/(2π). For a discrete level at fixed lab angle: √E = √Ec cosθ ± √(E′ − Ec sin²θ), dσ/dΩlab = g(μ′) E / (√E′ · |√E − √Ec cosθ|)/(2π); both branches appear when E′ < Ec. Forward-emitted particles are faster than backward ones, so the spectrum depends on the angle. Masses are the neutron-mass ratios of the file (At = AWR, Ap = AWP); non-relativistic.

“As filed” mode. Uses the frame stated by the file (LCT 1 = lab, 2 = CM, 3 = lab for products with A ≤ 4 and CM for heavier ones): lab data are used as they are, so the spectral shape is the same at every angle and only its magnitude changes with the angular factor g(μ). Use this mode to check the evaluation, or when the evaluator already included the kinematic shift.
