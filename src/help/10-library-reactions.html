10. Library reactions (p, d, t, ³He, α): reaction probability, yields and secondary spectra (how to use)

  1. Choose p, d, t, ³He or α as the beam particle. The panel Nuclear reaction data (ENDF-6) appears on the left and uses the library of that projectile (TENDL-p, -d, -t, -h or -a). Everything below (plots, tables, secondary spectra) works identically for the five projectiles; the symbol of the projectile replaces α in the labels, e.g. (p,n) or (d,α), and “per incident p”.
  2. Choose the library folder: the TENDL2025 folder, which holds the sub-folders TENDL-a, TENDL-p, TENDL-d, TENDL-t and TENDL-h (the sub-folders are searched), or a single one of them. The files of the sublibrary of the projectile selected are used (prefix a-, p-, d-, t-, h- for α, p, d, t, ³He); changing the beam particle switches library without choosing the folder again. One file per isotope, named a-Ar040.tendl: element symbol + three-digit mass number; files of isomers such as …m are ignored). Other ENDF-6 libraries are read in the same way when the file name starts with the projectile letter: JENDL-5 (and IAEA) files such as d_003-Li-006.dat (p_…, d_…, a_…), also d-Li006.endf or d-Li-6.endf. They may be inside a folder or chosen directly with Choose file(s)…. The JENDL-5 files give the total reaction cross section (MF 3 / MT 5) and, in MF 6 / MT 5, the multiplicity of every product (n, p, d, t, ³He, α, γ and the residual nuclei); the production cross sections (Production cross sections card, activation) are σMT5(E) × multiplicity(E). ENDF/B-VIII.0 files such as d-003_Li_006.endf (MT 2, 50 = (d,n0), 600 = (d,p0), 800 = (d,α0)) are read too; these evaluations end at 5 MeV (d + Li-6) and the library cross sections are zero above their last energy, which the panel reports when the beam energy is higher. The same holds for the JENDL-5 proton and α libraries (p_003-Li-007.dat, a_013-Al-027.dat); the neutron production of the α files (MT 201, (α,xn), cross section in MF 3 and distribution in MF 6) is merged into the total-reaction channel MT 5 as a neutron multiplicity σ201/σ5, so that neutron yields, spectra and activation use it like any other product. Their laboratory energy-angle tables (MF 6, LAW 7) are used for the secondary-particle spectra and angular distributions (n, p, d, t, ³He, α) in the Reactions tab, like the continuum distributions of TENDL; the heavy residual nuclei have only multiplicities in these files, so no recoil spectra are available for them. Only the names of the files are indexed; a file is opened only if its isotope is in the target (natural or edited abundances), when you press Run simulation or Re-read target isotopes. Isotopes without a file keep the built-in model cross section. Only the files of the target isotopes are ever opened; the browser’s folder dialog just lists the file names. The files follow the materials: when you edit a layer (other element or isotopes) or change the beam particle, the files of the new isotopes are read automatically; no need to choose anything again. Chromium-based browsers remember the folder. Inside the Claude viewer the folder is chosen through the file dialog.
  3. Saving the spectra. Every secondary-particle plot has its own Save this plot buttons (PNG, SVG, ASCII, CSV, ROOT file), and above the first plot Save all spectra stores all of them as shown (hidden angles, log axis and zoom included): PNG and SVG as a zip with one file per plot, ASCII and CSV as one file with a block per plot, ROOT as one file with all histograms (names prefixed p1_, p2_ … by plot).
  4. Non-elastic cross section for the transport (tick box, on by default): the removal of the projectiles uses MT 3 of the library instead of the model.
  5. Run, then open the tab Reactions (ENDF). Controls: Reaction channels (menu with tick boxes, one or several; All reaction channels is the sum of the non-overlapping channels), Plot (reaction probability dP/dE or cross section σ(E)), Layer, Energy unit, Bins, and Secondary energies from (two-body kinematics, default, or the lab double-differential data as filed).
  6. Read the results, from top to bottom: (a) the reaction-probability plot (one curve per layer, or per channel when several are ticked); (b) one plot per layer and secondary particle (n, p, d, t, ³He, α, photons and the recoil nuclei, all residual nuclei of the selected channels summed) with the energy distribution at the laboratory angles 0°, 5°, 10°, 30°, 60°, 120°, 160° (per primary, per MeV and per sr) and the distribution integrated over all angles, shown divided by 4π so that it is also per primary, per MeV and per sr (the angle-averaged value: equal to the angle curves for isotropic emission; multiply by 4π to get dN/dE; the saved CSV/ASCII/ROOT files contain what is plotted). Every one of these plots can be zoomed (drag a box, double-click resets), has its own linear/logarithmic Y axis, and angles are hidden or shown by clicking the legend; (c) the table with the reaction probability and the yields of n, p, d, t, ³He, α and photons per incident α, per layer, channel and isotope, plus the residual nuclei and a consistency line.

The line under each secondary plot says which data were used: the kinematics mode and the kind of angular data found in the file (Legendre, Kalbach-Mann with a from the file or from systematics, two-body discrete level, phase space, isotropic).

Neutron counting. The collapsible card Neutron counting (below Irradiation and activation and Neutron counting, above Production cross sections) gives the neutrons emitted by the beam in the reactions of the loaded library, per steradian, at chosen laboratory angles. It has its own copies of the activation inputs Beam current, Current unit, Charge state q, Irradiation time (with unit) and Layer (one layer or all; the neutrons of the layers are added), so the two cards can be set independently. Angles. The menu ENDF angle lists the cos θ values (converted to θ) of the tabulated neutron distributions of the files (JENDL-5 LAW 7 tables); Add this angle adds one, Add all ENDF angles adds the whole grid. Any angle θ (0 – 180°, laboratory, 0° = forward along the beam) adds a free angle: between two ENDF angles the result is interpolated with the interpolation laws written in the file — the cos θ law of the LAW 7 table, the law between the incident energies (both read from the TAB2 records, ENDF-6 codes 1 histogram, 2 linear-linear, 3 linear-log, 4 log-linear, 5 log-log) and the law of the emitted-energy tables (TAB1) — and the table “Angular data” says whether a row is an ENDF node or interpolated. Libraries without tabulated angles (TENDL Legendre / Kalbach-Mann, two-body kinematics) are evaluated analytically at any angle (“analytic”). The default angles are 0, 30, 60, 90, 120, 150 and 180° (the nearest ENDF angles when the file has a grid); the chips above the plots remove angles and Default angles restores them. Quantities (menu Quantity in the plots; the table always shows all of them): the neutron rate in n s⁻¹ sr⁻¹ (ions per second = current / (q·e), times the neutrons per ion and sr), the total number of neutrons n sr⁻¹ produced during the irradiation time (rate × time), and the neutrons per unit of charge n sr⁻¹ µC⁻¹, which is the same number as n s⁻¹ sr⁻¹ per µA of beam (it does not depend on the current or on the irradiation time). The last row of the table (“4π”) integrates over all directions. The flight path, the time resolution and the detector are set in the detector window (next paragraph); the spectra of this card are always the ideal spectra emitted from the target at each angle — no flight path, no detector, no resolution. Plots (standard controls: log/linear axes, zoom, legend, PNG/PDF/SVG/ASCII/CSV/ROOT saving): (1) the neutron energy spectrum at each selected angle, per MeV; (2) the angular distribution of the neutrons against cos θ: the yield per steradian integrated over all neutron energies at each angle (the curve itself covers every angle from backward to forward), with the selected angles marked; forward is cos θ = +1. The line under the table compares the integral of the plotted angular distribution over the sphere with the total from the energy spectrum (a consistency check, a few per cent at most) and reports channels with neutrons but no distribution in the file, which are not counted.

Configure neutron detector. The highlighted button Configure neutron detector in the Neutron counting card opens a window with a sketch of the geometry (point source, flight path L, cylinder of diameter D and thickness t on the beam axis) and the parameters: scintillator (BC-501A / EJ-301 / NE-213 liquid, ρ = 0.874 g/cm³, H/C = 1.212; EJ-309, 0.959 g/cm³, 1.25; stilbene C₁₄H₁₂, 1.16 g/cm³, H/C = 0.857, with the light-output curve of the NE-213 type liquids as an approximation; BC-400 plastic, 1.032 g/cm³, 1.103; density and H/C stay editable), diameter and thickness (cm; default 10 cm × 10 cm), threshold (keVee, default 100), flight path L (m, default 25) and time resolution (ns FWHM, default 3), the histories per energy and the largest energy of the efficiency curve (default 200 MeV; the curve is used up to the highest energy of the neutron spectrum, above that its last value is kept). Under Light-output curves the parameters of the proton curve Lp = a1·E − a2·[1 − exp(−a3·Ea4)] (Cecil et al. for the liquids, Madey / Verbinski for the plastic), of the α curve and of the carbon-recoil curve can be edited; carbon light can be switched off. Model. Neutrons from the point source enter the front face (weight ∝ solid angle); the free path follows the macroscopic cross section of H and C; at each collision the target and channel are drawn — ¹H(n,n) (CM angular distribution of the file; isotropic below 10 MeV in the built-in data), ¹²C(n,n), the ¹²C inelastic levels (γ rays escape; levels above 7.27 MeV and the continuum break up into 3α), (n,p), (n,α) and the other charged-particle channels — the recoil energy is converted into light with the curve of the particle, and the neutron continues with its new energy and direction until it escapes, is absorbed or falls below 10 keV. The light of all collisions of one neutron is summed (multiple scattering), and the event counts when the total exceeds the threshold. Efficiency = detected / neutrons entering the front face, with the binomial error; the single-collision ¹H(n,n) estimate is plotted for comparison. Cross sections. No files need to be loaded: the cross sections and the CM angular distribution of the elastic channels of ¹H (ENDF/B-VIII.0 1001.800nc) and ¹²C (6012.800nc, with the reaction channels MT 28, 51–62, 91, 102, 103, 104, 107, up to 150 MeV) are built into the program; they are the room-temperature (293.6 K) ACE files of the lib80x library (Conlin et al., LA-UR-18-24034), stored with 5 significant digits. Only that temperature is used. Above 20 MeV: the ¹H table of the library ends at 20 MeV, so above it the n-p cross section follows an evaluated-data table (20 – 250 MeV, normalised at 20 MeV) and the CM angular distribution of 20 MeV is multiplied by 1 + b·μ² with b = 0.025·(E − 20 MeV), at most 3 (forward-backward peaking; approximate); for ¹²C the discrete-level cross sections end at 20 MeV and the library gives the nonelastic cross section as a single lumped reaction (MT 5), which the model treats as one break-up-type channel with Q ≈ −12 MeV (alpha / proton / neutron sharing the energy). The library has no discrete levels above 20 MeV, so the efficiency above 20 MeV is less accurate (a small step of a few per cent at 20 MeV is a model artefact); for high-energy beams load-quality data (JENDL/HE, TENDL, LA150) would be needed. Plots (standard controls; the menus Angle and Distribution above the third plot choose the emission angle of the card and energy or time of flight for the two distributions): (1) the efficiency curve; (2) the expected detected distribution — the ideal spectrum of that angle × solid angle of the front face × efficiency(E), in the quantity chosen in the card, with the time resolution folded in (a Gaussian whose energy width follows from the time resolution at the flight path); (3) the reconstructed energy or time-of-flight spectrum and (4) the pulse-height spectrum, both from one full-statistics Monte Carlo of 300000 neutron histories whose energies are drawn from the ideal spectrum of that angle: every history that gives a pulse above the threshold is counted at its time of flight over L smeared with the Gaussian time resolution (the energy is then reconstructed from that time of flight), and every history with light is entered in the pulse-height spectrum (all events and events above the threshold). The reconstructed and the expected distributions agree within the Monte Carlo statistics; the reconstructed one shows the true event-by-event response. Detected neutrons in the counting card. Once the efficiency has been calculated, the card Neutron counting shows a third plot, Neutrons detected by the configured detector: for every selected angle the ideal spectrum × the solid angle of the front face × the intrinsic efficiency ε(E) (events above the threshold / neutrons entering the front face), with the time resolution folded in, as energy or time-of-flight spectrum (menu Distribution). Its integral is the total number of neutrons detected, listed in the table below it with the Monte Carlo uncertainty. The pulse-height spectrum remains in the detector window. Statistics. The table Statistics of the detected neutrons gives, for the selected angle, the neutrons reaching the front face, the detected neutrons with their Monte Carlo uncertainty (1σ, from the weighted ratio estimator of the 300000 histories, falling as 1/√N), the mean efficiency, the number of detected events and effective histories, the same number from the efficiency curve and, for the total-number quantity, the Poisson counting uncertainty √N of a real measurement. Limits: the light-output curves of different publications differ by 10 – 20 % at low energy, which moves the efficiency near the threshold; the α and carbon curves are simple power laws (adjust them to your calibration); neutron scattering in the photomultiplier, housing and light guide, the escape of recoil protons through the walls and the optical resolution of the light output are not included; the γ rays of inelastic scattering are not detected; (n,2n) and capture give no light.

Production cross sections. The collapsible card Production cross sections (below Irradiation and activation) plots σ(E) for the production of any residual nucleus as a function of the energy of the incident particle, straight from the library read in the panel Nuclear reaction data (ENDF-6); it needs no simulation run. Besides residual nuclei, emitted particles can be plotted: type n (neutrons), p, d, t, h (³He), a (α) or g (γ) to get σprod(E) = Σ σchannel(E) × multiplicity(E) over the primary channels, for example the neutron production of d + Li-7 from the MF 6 multiplicity of the JENDL-5 file (He-3 still means the residual nucleus). Type the product (Ar-37 or 37Ar for all states, Cl-38m, Cl-38m2 for an isomer, Cl-38g for the ground state), choose All layers or one layer and press Add to the plot; as many curves as you like can be added and removed with the × of their label. σ is the sum of the primary channels (MF 3 cross section × MF 6 residual yield, with the isomeric fractions of the MF 6 / MF 10 data), the same channels the activation uses, in mb per atom of the selected layer(s): the target isotopes are weighted by their atom fraction. The energy axis runs from 0 to the beam energy unless Maximum energy is given. σ is evaluated at Number of energy points equidistant energies (default 300; raise it to follow sharp structures, lower it to smooth the curve), or, with the option use the energy points of the library, exactly at the energy points of the library channels that produce the nucleus. The plot has the standard controls (linear/logarithmic X and Y, drag-zoom, Reset zoom, legend, hover values, Save plot as PNG, PDF, SVG, ASCII, CSV, ROOT file and ROOT macro).
