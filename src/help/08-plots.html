8. The plots

Stopping power & range — Analytic curves for the beam particle in one layer or in all layers: total, electronic, nuclear (ions) and radiative (electrons) stopping power, or the CSDA range, versus energy. Units: MeV cm²/g, keV/μm, MeV/cm, eV/10¹⁵ atoms/cm²; energy axis in MeV or MeV/u; the dashed line marks the beam energy. It needs no simulation, and is the quickest way to compare materials.

Stopping-power data — Choose the data set under Stopping powers and models → Stopping-power data. Newest available (default): ICRU Report 90 (2016) for protons (and d, t), ⁴He/³He, ¹²C and electrons in liquid water, dry air and graphite (for electrons only when the density is within 15 % of the reference: 1 g/cm³, 1.205 mg/cm³, 1.7 g/cm³); the 2005 update of ICRU 73 for Li–Ar ions in water and air; elsewhere the NIST PSTAR (p, d, t), ASTAR (³He, ⁴He) and ICRU 73 (Li–Ar) tables. NIST PSTAR / ASTAR + ICRU 73: those older tables everywhere. Semi-empirical: Bethe–Bloch with Andersen–Ziegler fits and effective charges, no tables (useful to see how far a model is from the data). Tables cover 25 elements and ~50 ICRU/NIST compounds (a compound table is used automatically when the composition matches, e.g. water, air, Mylar, Kapton, polyethylene, PMMA…; otherwise Bragg additivity). Elements absent from a table are interpolated in Z, heavier ions (Z > 18) are scaled from the Ar-ion table at equal reduced velocity. Outside the tables: ∝ v below, Bethe–Bloch above. Electron radiative stopping is always the built-in formula. Nuclear stopping is ZBL: hard recoils are simulated explicitly (they give the low-energy tail), the soft part is continuous. Tick compare the stopping-power data sets in “Stopping power & range” to overlay the three.

Which stopping-power data set is more realistic? Newest available, wherever it applies. ICRU 90 (2016) replaces the mean excitation energies of ICRU 49 / PSTAR / ASTAR by newer values (liquid water 78 eV instead of 75 eV, graphite 81 eV) and revises the shell and density-effect corrections. The change is small but systematic: in the tables of this app the ICRU 90 stopping power of liquid water is lower than PSTAR/ASTAR by about 0.5 % at 50–100 MeV, 0.7–0.8 % at 5–10 MeV and 1–1.5 % at 0.5–2 MeV (p and α alike), which makes the CSDA range of a 10 MeV proton about 0.8 % longer and that of a 100 MeV proton about 0.5 % longer; for graphite the differences are similar above 1 MeV and grow to a few % below ~0.3 MeV; for dry air the two sets agree. In every other material (metals, plastics, compounds, gases other than air, and all Li–Ar ions outside water and air) the two options use the same tables, so they give identical results. Neither set is “the truth” for solids: below ~1 MeV/u the uncertainty of the data (5–10 %), phase and chemical-binding effects, and the dependence on the adopted I-value are larger than the difference between the two sets. Use Newest available for new calculations (dosimetry, hadron therapy, water or air targets); use NIST PSTAR / ASTAR + ICRU 73 to reproduce older published numbers, PSTAR/ASTAR benchmarks or codes that still rely on ICRU 49 (SRIM comparisons, older Geant4 physics lists); use Semi-empirical only to see how far the model is from the tables. The check box compare the stopping-power data sets overlays them, so you can see the size of the effect for your own material and energy.

Depth profile — Energy loss per unit depth, per primary, versus z, with layer boundaries shaded. Total deposited is all energy that stays in the material at that depth (including the δ-electrons’ energy); lost by primary is what the projectile itself loses; the ion-electronic, ion-nuclear and δ-electron parts can be shown separately. The Bragg peak of ions appears here; for electrons the broad maximum is visible.

Stopping positions — Histograms of where primaries stop: z (projected range and range straggling), x, y (lateral) and path length. Choose stopped particles or all primaries. The table gives N, mean ± error, σ, FWHM, median, min and max; the mean of z is the projected range, the path-length mean is the real range.

Energy loss per layer — For one layer: the distribution, over the primaries, of the energy lost by the primary or of the energy deposited (δ-electrons included). The vertical line is the mean; the most probable value is given under the plot. In thin layers the shape is asymmetric (Landau/Vavilov-like with a high-energy tail), in thick layers it approaches a Gaussian. “Only those that lose energy” removes particles that did not interact in the layer.

Radial & lateral — Looks at the plane z = depth. Set the depth with the box or slider (in the chosen length unit). Modes: radial distribution r from the undeflected beam axis, radial density (per area), x or y distributions, energy spectrum at depth (kinetic energy of the ions when they cross the plane — set the depth to a layer face for the energy entering or leaving it), lateral spread vs depth (σx, σy, r rms and mean r along z) and fraction reaching depth (penetration curve). The Gaussian / Rayleigh overlay uses the measured σ, which makes the non-Gaussian single-scattering tails visible. Only primaries that cross the plane are counted; the note under the plot gives how many.

Ion flux — Energy distribution of the ion flux Φ(E), in units of the incident flux Φ₀ per energy unit (1/MeV, 1/keV…; dimensionless apart from the energy bin): inside the selected layer (or all layers combined), every layer overlaid, and at the exit of all layers (transmitted ions, exact). Inside a layer Φ(E) is the track-length flux: total ion path length in the layer divided by its thickness, so ∫Φ dE = 1 for an undisturbed beam at normal incidence and falls below 1 where ions stop or are removed. At the exit it is the number of transmitted ions per incident ion per energy unit. The table lists ∫Φ dE, mean energy and σ for each curve. Only primary ions are included.

Reactions (ENDF) — Reaction probability, yields and energy-angle distributions of the secondary particles for p, d, t, ³He and α projectiles, computed from the TENDL ENDF-6 library of the projectile. Described in sections 10 (how to use it) and 11 (how it is computed).

Knock-on electrons — Energy spectrum of the δ-electrons produced above the threshold, per layer or for all layers, as dN/dT or dN/dlnT per primary (log–log by default). Below the threshold the energy is in the continuous loss.

Collapsing panels — The panels Interactions with the target layers and Irradiation and activation, Neutron counting and Production cross sections fold away when you click their heading (▾/▸), also with Enter or Space on the focused heading; the state is remembered. A collapsed panel is still updated in the background.

Irradiation and activation (panel below “Interactions with the target layers”) — Appears for p, d, t, ³He and α beams once the library is loaded and a run is done. Inputs: beam current (particles/s, pA, nA, μA, mA), charge state of the ions (blank = fully stripped), irradiation time, cooling time, layer, and the decay data: the internal database or ENDF-6 decay files loaded by the user. Results: the total activity; one plot per layer of the grow-in and decay of the strongest nuclei and of the whole decay chain during the irradiation and the cooling (drag to zoom, double-click to reset, click a legend entry to show/hide a nucleus; PNG, SVG, ASCII, CSV for each plot or for all layers); and a table of the residual nuclei and their decay products with half-life, decay modes and branching, origin (direct or ingrowth), production rate, atoms and activity at the end of the irradiation and after cooling (CSV). The inputs are saved with the setup. Described in section 11.6. When decay data with γ lines are loaded (ENDF-6 files), a second plot below the decay curve of every layer shows the γ emission rate (γ/s, discrete lines of the decay data) of the strongest nuclides and their total. Every plot of this panel has Unselect all / Select all buttons next to its legend, to hide or show all the nuclides at once (a single nuclide is toggled by clicking its legend entry).

Nuclear reactions — Depth distribution of the nuclear interactions and a per-layer table (model cross section, or the non-elastic cross section of the library for p, d, t, ³He and α).

Tracks — x–z, y–z or r–z projections of the first primaries (red) and their δ-electrons (grey) over the layers. The lateral and depth scales differ unless “equal axis scales” is ticked, so scattering is exaggerated by default.
