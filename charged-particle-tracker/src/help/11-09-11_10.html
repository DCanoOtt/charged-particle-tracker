11.10 Simulated decay measurement (HPGe). Button Simulate decay measurement in the activation panel (next to Configure the Ge detector and DAQ). It simulates counting selected γ lines with an HPGe detector during the irradiation and the cooling, with the production rates (beam current of the activation panel) and decay chains of the layers of the target. It needs ENDF-6 decay data (discrete γ lines).

  • Controls: irradiation time and cooling time, each with its own time unit (s, min, hours, days, years) and its own points per time unit; a time unit for the plot axis (automatic = the unit of the longer phase; it only changes the display, CSV and ROOT axes, not the calculation); ROI half-width (× FWHM, default 1.5). Example: irradiation 30 min at 0.2 points per minute (a point every 5 min) and cooling 2 hours at 3 points per hour (a point every 20 min). One point = the counts of one time bin; the bins of the irradiation and those of the cooling are separate (the end of the irradiation is always a bin edge); at most 1000 bins in total (both phases are scaled down together if the request is larger). Simulate Experiment runs the calculation. Afterwards every change of a parameter (times, selection, background, Ge/DAQ configuration, uncertainties) recalculates automatically.
  • Select isotopes and γ lines (highlighted button): opens a window with the nuclides of the decay chains classified by layer (the 40 strongest per layer, by activity × γ yield) and their discrete lines (filter by minimum Iγ). The selector Layers shown chooses all layers or only one, and it also sets which layers the detector counts (there is no separate setting; the scope is shown next to the number of selected lines); Strongest line of each isotope, Select all (every line listed, i.e. with Iγ above the minimum, up to 14 per isotope) and Clear selection act on the layers shown. Tick one or several lines, from one or several layers; each gets its own ROI, colour and curves (named L1, L2, … when the target has several layers). The detector counts together the layers that have at least one selected line: their pulses are added in the pile-up rate and the γ lines of those layers that fall in a ROI count as neighbouring lines. The default is the strongest line of the three strongest nuclides.
  • Layer rule: if one layer is chosen, only the isotopes and lines selected in that layer are simulated and the selections made in the other layers are ignored (they are kept for later); the pile-up and the γ spectrum use only the decays of that layer. Only if all layers are chosen (two or more) are lines from different layers mixed; the detector then sees the sum of the layers (pile-up and spectrum). The counter in the selection window states how many lines are active in the chosen scope.
  • Bateman equations: the coupled chains (branchings, isomers, several parents of the same daughter) are solved with the same matrix-exponential method as the activation plots, at the edges of every time bin. It is stable for stiff chains (very different half-lives) and is recomputed whenever the times or the layer change. Decays in a bin = N(t₁) − N(t₂) + production in the bin + feeding from the parents' decays. Because the irradiation (production + decay) and the cooling (decay only) are different problems, the solver keeps separate time grids for the two phases: the irradiation and the cooling times may have different units, and different sampling does not change the physics, only where the populations are evaluated.
  • Counts: peak counts in a bin = decays × Iγ × εpeak(E) × fraction of the Gaussian peak inside the ROI. With count neighbouring γ lines the full-energy counts of the other lines of all nuclides that fall in the ROI are added (they remain in the net counts, as in a real measurement).
  • ROI and resolution: ROI = Eγ ± k·FWHM(Eγ), k = 1.5 by default (±3.2σ, 99.9 % of the peak); the window shows the calculation with an example at 661.7 keV and the table lists the ROI limits of each line. The resolution is that of the Ge/DAQ configuration, written as σ²(E) = a + b·E (constant FWHM: b = 0; √E model: a = 0). A worse resolution widens the ROI, so more background is collected and the signal-to-background ratio gets worse.
  • Detector efficiency and DAQ: efficiency, pulse width τ and pulses per full-energy count come from Configure the Ge detector and DAQ (constant, curve or Ge geometry model). With the Ge model the pulse rate uses the total efficiency.
  • Background: A = none; B = smooth exponential A·e−E/L (L editable, default 500 keV) times the low-energy efficiency ε(E), normalised to the total rate; C = B(E) = ε(E)·[A₁e−E/L₁ + A₂e−E/L₂] + Σ ε(Ei)·Ii/(σi√2π)·exp(−(E−Ei)²/2σi²), with L₁ = 150 keV (soft continuum, 70 % of the continuum), L₂ = 1500 keV (hard continuum), σi from the resolution and an editable list of peaks (Pb X-rays 72.8–87.3 keV, 212Pb, 214Pb, 214Bi, 228Ac, annihilation 511 keV, 40K 1460.8 keV, 208Tl 583.2 and 2614.5 keV, …; areas in counts/s for a total of 5 cps). The models extend from 0 to 3500 keV. Because the exponential shapes are empirical (fitted to what a detector records), the extrapolation to low energies includes a decreasing detection efficiency: ε(E) = exp(−(Ec/E)³)·(1−exp(−E/1.5 keV)), i.e. photoelectric absorption (∝ E−3) in the end cap, dead layer and contacts plus a soft electronic threshold. Ec (default 25 keV, 50 % transmission at ≈ 28 keV) is editable; ε → 1 above ≈ 100 keV and B(E) → 0 for E → 0. The total rate (counts/s between 0 and 3500 keV, default 5 cps, typical of a ~50 % detector in a modest shield) is adjustable; peaks and continuum scale with it, and the continuum is the total minus the peaks. Background in a ROI = ∫ROIB(E)dE × bin time. A preview plot (0–3500 keV) shows the model. The default areas are representative, not measured values: replace them with your own background spectrum.
  • Net counts and statistics: total counts in the ROI T and background counts B are drawn from Poisson distributions around their expectations (the background as an independent measurement of the same duration); net = T − B and σstat = √(T + B). Without Poisson fluctuations the expectations are shown. The random seed makes a run reproducible; a new seed is drawn at every Simulate Experiment unless new seed is unticked.
  • Pile-up: first order (Poisson), as in section 11.8: the peak counts are multiplied by exp(−2Rτ), R = total pulse rate of the bin: all γ lines of all nuclides of the layer (not only the selected lines), with the pulses per decay of the efficiency model, plus the background; if lines of two or more layers are selected, the rates of those layers are added. The hover note shows this total detector rate. The pile-up level reported is 1 − exp(−2Rτ). The loss can be switched off with apply pile-up losses; sum peaks falling into the ROI are not added.
  • Uncertainty of the beam current and of the Ge efficiency: two relative uncertainties (%, 0 = none). They never change the counts (total, background, net, expected values, spectra): they only enlarge the error bars. The two terms are fully correlated between bins and lines, so they are propagated on the net counts as a relative uncertainty: σ = √(σstat² + (δI² + δε²)·net²), with σstat = √(T + B); error bars and tooltip show the total and the two parts.
  • Plot: counts vs time with total counts in the ROI (circles) and net counts (squares) per selected line; vertical error bars (±1σ) and horizontal bars showing the width of the time bin of each point (both can be switched off); theoretical (noise-free) expected curves for the total (dashed; option to include or exclude the background and the pile-up) and for the net counts; vertical line at the end of the irradiation; legend with show/hide per curve, select/unselect all, linear/log Y axis. Zoom as in the other plots: drag a box (a thin band zooms one axis), double-click or Reset zoom resets; Shift+drag pans. The mouse wheel does not zoom (it scrolls the window).
  • Legend and rates: the legend has one entry per selected γ line, which shows or hides all its curves (total, net and expected); the Show boxes switch the kinds of curve. The selector Plot switches the vertical axis between counts per bin and count rates (counts/s) = counts / live time of the bin (errors scale the same way).
  • Hover: pointing at a point shows its information in an independent floating window: the lower-left corner of the window is at the cursor plus a small shift to the right and upwards, so the circle that marks the point and the cursor stay visible (the window flips to the other side near the screen edges). It lists the decaying isotope, γ energy and ROI, phase (irradiation or cooling), time and bin limits, total counts in the ROI, background counts (and the expected value), net counts ± σ (absolute and %, split into statistical and systematic parts), the corresponding count rates (total, background and net/peak, in counts/s) and the pile-up level.
  • γ-ray spectrum of a time range: press Select a time range → γ spectrum and drag over the plot of points (or type the limits; Whole run takes everything). The limits snap to the nearest time-bin edges. Below the plot the simulated spectrum of the detector in that range appears, with all the γ lines of all the nuclides of the chosen layer (all layers if so chosen), not only the selected lines. It is the Ge-detector simulation of section 11.8: full-energy peaks with the chosen energy resolution, single Compton continuum, escape peaks and double Compton (two scatterings in the crystal), all weighted by the efficiency of the chosen model (Ge geometry, curve ε(E) with total and peak columns, or constant: no continuum), the selected background model (A, B or C) integrated over the live time of the range, the first-order pile-up factor averaged over the bins of the range (weighted by the counting rate), the sampled beam-current and efficiency factors if switched on, and Poisson fluctuations in every 1 keV channel (the same random seed as the run): the signal (peaks, Compton, double Compton) and the background of any model (B or C) fluctuate independently, and the total is their sum; the background curve shows its own fluctuating realisation (the CSV also has the expected background). At the first display only the total spectrum and the background are shown; the full-energy peaks, the single Compton + escape peaks and the double Compton are off by default and can be switched on in the legend. The legend shows or hides each component (total with fluctuations, peaks, single Compton + escapes, double Compton, background); linear or log vertical axis; Upper energy (0 = automatic, above the highest γ line); zoom as in the other plots; PNG, SVG, PDF and CSV of the spectrum. The line above the plot gives the total number of counts in the spectrum (± √N) and the average count rate (counts / live time), also in the CSV header. The selected γ lines are marked with coloured dotted vertical lines labelled with the nuclide and energy (the colour is that of the line in the point plot; switch with mark the selected γ lines). The save buttons of the point plot are directly under that plot; those of the spectrum are under the spectrum. The spectrum updates when the run is recalculated.
  • Saving: PNG, SVG and PDF of the plot; CSV and ASCII (tab-separated) with all bins of all selected lines (time, counts, count rates, background, net, σ total/statistical/systematic, pile-up, expected values, ROI); ROOT file (.root) with, for each isotope-γ combination, the histograms of the total counts, background, net counts (with errors), their count rates (total, background, net), expected net counts and pile-up level against time.
  • Limits: point source and Gaussian peaks (no tailing, no Compton continuum of the sample in the ROI); constant efficiency over the ROI; the background model is generic; the counting rate is assumed to be dead-time-free apart from the first-order pile-up; the ROOT file is written by the application itself (no ROOT installation needed).
