11.8 Pile-up in the γ-spectrum window. Two pulses that reach the electronics closer than the pulse width τ (resolving time of the shaper, in μs) cannot be separated: they are recorded as one pulse of the sum height or are rejected, so the peaks lose counts and sum peaks appear. The window gives the probability of that for the slice that is open. Assumptions: the pulses arrive randomly (Poisson), with a rate that is the detected rate n of the slice multiplied by the factor “pulses per full-energy count” f (f = 1 counts only the events in the peaks, which the spectrum contains; the Compton events of a real detector also make pulses, so f is the total-to-peak ratio, ≥ 1). What is “pulses per full-energy count”? It is the number of pulses the detector gives for every count that ends up in a full-energy peak: f = εtot/εpeak, the ratio of the total efficiency (any interaction in the crystal, which produces a pulse) to the full-energy efficiency. A γ that deposits only part of its energy (Compton scattering, escape) is not in a peak but still makes a pulse that can overlap another one, so the pulse rate is n = (full-energy rate) × f. It is 1 at best (every interaction a full absorption, as in a very large crystal or at very low energy) and typically 3–10 for a Ge detector at 1 MeV (peak-to-total ratio of 0.1–0.3). With the constant and curve efficiency models you type f (the default 1 underestimates the pile-up; use about 1/(peak-to-total) of your detector); with the Ge detector model it is calculated from the geometry, see section 11.9. For a pulse rate n (s−1):

  • μ = n·τ: mean number of other pulses inside a window of width τ.
  • Ppile-up = 1 − e−2μ: probability that a given pulse has another one within ±τ, before (leading) or after (trailing) it. The probability that a pulse is clean is e−2μ. If only the pulses that follow it count (trailing pile-up, a pulse that is not preceded by another one), P = 1 − e−μ; this one-sided value is not shown in the window, take it from μ. For μ ≪ 1, P ≈ 2μ.
  • Pairs recorded as one pulse: ∫ n(t)²·τ dt over the slice. The rate n is taken from the activities at the two slice limits (n0, n1, linear in between): pairs = τ·Tlive·(n0² + n0n1 + n1²)/3. The window shows the pulse rates at the start, at the end and the mean, μ and P for the mean and for the start of the slice (the worst case, since the activities decay). Slices should be short enough that n does not change much.
  • Spectrum with pile-up (option “add the pile-up spectrum”): Spu(E) = e−2μ̄·S(E) + (pairs/f²)·(p ⊛ p)(E), with p = S/ΣS the normalised detected spectrum and ⊛ the convolution (the sum of the two energies, 1 keV channels). The first term is the part of every peak that stays clean (μ̄ from the mean rate); the second one the sum peaks and the sum continuum of the pairs in which both pulses are full-energy events (a fraction 1/f² of the pairs). The pile-up pulses are also shown alone. The Gaussian resolution is already in S, so the sum peaks are wider by √2.
