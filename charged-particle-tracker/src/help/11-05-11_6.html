11.6 Activation and decay chains. The residual nuclei and their yields Y per primary are those of the table “Everything produced” (all non-overlapping channels, MF 6 residual-nucleus entries). With the beam current I (A) and the charge state q of the ions (default: fully stripped, q = Z of the projectile), the primaries per second are n = I/(q e); a current given in particles/s is used directly. The production rate of a nuclide is R = Y·n (atoms/s), constant during the irradiation; every layer is treated separately.

Decay chains. Yes: the grow-in and decay of the whole decay chain is followed. For all products and all their daughters, down to stable nuclei, the Bateman system Ni′ = Ri + Σp bp→i λp Np − λi Ni (λ = ln 2/T½, b = branching fraction of the decay mode that leads from p to i) is solved exactly with the matrix-exponential propagator, first with the production on (irradiation) and then with R = 0 (cooling); the activity is A = λN. Plots: the quantity can be the activity of the radioactive nuclei or the number of atoms of all isotopes produced during the irradiation and the cooling (stable isotopes included: direct production and ingrowth from the decay of the chain). Time grids: the irradiation and the cooling have separate grids, each built from the length of its own phase (irradiation T, cooling tc, so changing the cooling time rebuilds the cooling grid): the smallest step is h = T/222 (resp. tc/222); the propagators exp(A·2jh) are obtained by repeated squaring and the output times are n·h with n spaced geometrically (6 points per octave, so that half-lives from milliseconds to years are all resolved), plus 48 uniform points and the times ½, 1, 2, 3 and 5 half-lives of the 30 leading nuclei of the chain (about 200 points per phase). All points are exact solutions (no time-stepping error). Numerics: the system matrix is lower triangular (parents first) and the propagators are non-negative compartment-model matrices, so the exponential is computed by a Taylor series of the scaled matrix (norm ≤ 1/8) and squaring, with the diagonal of every propagator set to its exact value exp(−λt) after each squaring (no accumulation of rounding error; relative accuracy of about 1e-12 in the tests), denormals flushed, and the source normalised to the largest production rate. Only nuclides with λh ≥ 105 (shorter than ~10−5 of the smallest step) are taken in secular equilibrium with their parents, their activity being equal to their feeding. The option “Follow the decay chains” can be switched off to see the nuclei produced directly only. Not included: isomeric states of the products (the production is assigned to the ground state; with ENDF-6 decay data the isomers of the daughters are followed, e.g. IT of 110mAg), burn-up of the target, reactions of secondary particles, beam-current variations.

Decay-data source (selectable). (1) Internal database (default): nuclide table of the Python package mendeleev 1.3.0 (isotope and isotope-decay-mode tables; 3,557 nuclides; half-lives and decay modes from the NUBASE evaluation, Kondev et al., Chin. Phys. C 45 (2021) 030001, as redistributed by mendeleev). Only ground states, only the half-life, the decay modes and their intensities are used; the daughters follow from the mode (β−: Z+1; EC/β+: Z−1; α: Z−2, A−4; p, n, 2n, β−n … by the corresponding shifts); modes without a measured intensity are ignored when another one has a value, a nuclide whose modes have no intensities decays 100 % by its first listed mode; spontaneous fission and cluster decays are losses (no daughters). Decay energies and radiation emission are not part of the table. (2) Uploaded ENDF-6 decay data: choose a folder or several files (or one file with many materials) of an evaluated decay sub-library (e.g. ENDF/B-VIII.0 decay, JEFF-3.3/4.0 radioactive decay data, JENDL-5 decay data). The files are only indexed by their names when you choose them (names such as dec-027_Co_060.endf, dec-052_Te_127m1.endf, Co-60.endf, Te127m.dat); only the files of the nuclides that appear in the chains (the products, then step by step their daughters; stable nuclides need no file) are read, the others never. Files without a nuclide name (one big file with many materials) are read completely. Any file with a section MF 8, MT 457 is read: the half-life T½, the isomeric state (LISO), the stability flag (NST) and, for each decay mode, RTYP (1 β−, 2 EC/β+, 3 IT, 4 α, 5 n, 6 SF, 7 p, also multi-step modes such as 1.5 = β− followed by n), the isomeric state RFS of the daughter and the branching ratio BR. With “internal values for nuclides missing in the files” ticked, a nuclide that is not in the uploaded files is taken from the internal database; otherwise the chain stops there and the nuclide is listed as “not in table”. The source actually used for each run is stated above the plots.

(3) Uploaded ENSDF data (cross-check library). Choose the folder with the ENSDF mass-chain files (ensdf.001 … ensdf.300, one file per mass number A) or individual files, with the same “Choose folder…” / “Choose files…” buttons: the format is the one selected in Decay data (Uploaded ENSDF mass-chain files for ENSDF, Uploaded ENDF-6 decay data for ENDF/B, JEFF, JENDL; with the internal database the buttons are disabled). Each library keeps its own data, so you can switch between them without reloading. It replaces the other libraries completely (half-lives, branches, isomers, γ lines), so that the same irradiation can be evaluated with ENDF/B, ENSDF or the internal table and the results compared (activities, γ emission rate, spectra of the measurement window). The files are only indexed by their mass number; the chains A … A−5 (β, EC, IT in A; α, β−n, β+p … in A−1 … A−4) needed by a nuclide are read when it appears, each file once. How the data are read. Each decay dataset (e.g. “24MG 24NA B− DECAY (14.956 H)”) is the decay of one parent state (P record) into levels of the daughter (L records) with the β/EC/α feeding (B, E, A records) and the γ rays (G records). Photons per decay = RI · NR · BR (relative γ intensity × N-record normalisation × branching ratio of the mode; the PN record is used where it is filled); a γ ray with TI but no RI gives TI/(1+α). The branching ratios of the parent are the BR of its datasets (datasets without BR share what the others leave over). Isomers. The levels flagged M with T½ ≥ 1 ms are numbered by energy (m1, m2, …); the feeding of an isomeric daughter level (direct plus the γ feeding from above) is given to the isomer (decay chain!) and the γ rays emitted by the isomer itself are left to the decay of the isomer (its IT dataset), exactly as the ENDF decay files do; shorter-lived levels are part of the cascade. Annihilation photons: 2 × the β+ intensity, as a 511 keV line. β⁺ share. For an EC/β⁺ branch the fraction that decays by β⁺ is Σ IB(β⁺) / Σ (IB + IE) over the E records of the dataset, so the β emission rate and the annihilation photons use the evaluated value per nuclide; the “β⁺ share” field of the activation card is only used where the data do not give it (ENDF-6 files, internal table). Datasets with relative intensities only. If a dataset has neither an N record (NR) nor a PN record and its strongest γ ray has RI = 100, the intensities are relative and no absolute number of photons per decay can be derived: the γ lines of that dataset are left out (the decay modes and the feeding are still used). Transitions flagged E0 emit no photon (their energy goes to conversion electrons), so a nuclide whose only de-excitation is E0 (e.g. the 0⁺ → 0⁺ transitions of Pa-234m) has no γ line. A nuclide without photon lines shows no γ emission, but its decays and β emission are still counted. X-rays. ENSDF does not tabulate X-rays; they are computed. Every electron capture (E records; capture probabilities CK, CL from the continuation record, 0.86 and 0.12 of the captures if not given) and every internal conversion (G records: αK, αL from the continuation record KC, LC, L1C…, or, if only the total α is given, a typical split: 80 % K above the K edge) leaves a vacancy in the K or L shell of the daughter atom. A K vacancy emits the K X-rays with the fluorescence yield ωK and the line yields of the atomic data of the Elam/xraylib tables (data/xray.js, generated by tools/gen_xray.py; elements Z = 3 … 100; lines closer than 0.1 keV are merged) and leaves L vacancies (K→L X-rays; the Auger decay is assumed to leave 1.7 L vacancies); L vacancies are first redistributed by the Coster–Kronig transitions and then emit the L X-rays. M and higher shells are not followed, shake-off is neglected, and the intensities are approximate where CK/CL or the shell coefficients are missing (check: Cs-137 → Ba Kα1 32.19 keV 3.6 % per decay). The lines give the X-ray emission rate plot and, with “X-rays” switched on in the γ-spectrum window (default), they are lines of the simulated spectra, the pulse rate and the lines selectable in the measurement window. Status line. Below the Decay data menu the grey text lists the mass numbers A that have been read (e.g. “A = 22–24, 60”); if a nuclide is missing from the results, check there that its mass chain was read. ENSDF files do not tabulate conversion electrons or continuous spectra: the Compton background only has the discrete lines. Nuclides without a dataset as a parent (stable ones are recognised from the Adopted Levels, or from the internal table) use the internal values if the option internal values for nuclides missing in the files is set. Differences between the libraries in half-lives, branches and intensities are real differences of the evaluations (e.g. the ground-state half-life of ⁶⁰Mn: 0.28 s in ENSDF 2013, 51 s in ENDF/B-VIII.0).
