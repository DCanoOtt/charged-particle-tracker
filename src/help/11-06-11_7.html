11.7 Decays, γ emission and γ-ray spectra of a time slice. Summary text. Under the irradiation data the app gives the totals (activity, γ emission rate with discrete and continuous γ, X-ray emission rate, decays by mode, β emission rate) first for all layers together and then for each layer separately. Continuous γ emission. If the uploaded ENDF-6 decay data contain a continuous γ spectrum (spectrum type 0 with LCON = 1 or 2: the TAB1 RP(E) in 1/eV and the normalisation FC), it is used as N(E) = FC · RP(E) photons per decay and keV and is added to the γ emission rate (discrete + continuous), to the pulse rate (pile-up, count rates) and to the simulated Ge spectra. For the spectra it is cut into 2 keV pseudo-lines, each treated like a discrete line: its full-energy part is added to the full-energy-peak component (a smooth distribution, broadened by the resolution), and its single Compton continuum, escape peaks and double Compton to the Compton components. Hence the Compton background of the continuous emission is generated with the same Ge model as for the lines. The continuous part is not selectable as a γ line in the measurement window (it has no line energy) but it is counted in the ROIs as a smooth contribution and in the spectrum of the time range. The format of the continuous record is read from the standard layout (it is not tested against real files). The quantity X-ray emission rate, X/s = decays/s × the total intensity of the X-ray lines per decay, taken from the X-ray records (STYP 9, “X-rays and annihilation radiation”) of the uploaded ENDF-6 decay data, or computed from the electron-capture and conversion vacancies for ENSDF data (see the ENSDF paragraph above); it needs files that contain such records (many sublibraries do, e.g. the K X-rays after electron capture or internal conversion). It has one curve per nuclide and a dashed total. The 511 keV annihilation line of that record is not counted as an X-ray: it is added to the γ lines of the nuclide as a 511 keV line, so it enters the γ emission rate, the pulse rate and the simulated spectra (pure β⁺ emitters, which have no other γ line, then show their 2 × β⁺ annihilation photons). If the decay files have no such record, those nuclides still have no γ emission. Nuclides without X-ray records contribute nothing; if no nuclide has them, the plot falls back to the activity and says so in its title. The quantity Beta emission rate, β/s = decays/s × the number of β particles per decay: 1 for each β⁻ branch (including β⁻n, β⁻α), 2 for 2β⁻/2β⁺, and for the combined EC/β⁺ branches the share of positrons you enter in β⁺ share of the combined EC/β⁺ decays (default 0 %: the decay data do not separate EC from β⁺). Neutrons from β⁻n are not counted, and it needs no γ data. The info text above the plots gives the decay-mode balance (share of the decays/s by β⁻, EC/β⁺, α, IT/not specified, SF at the end of the irradiation) and the total β/s. The three quantities are different and are not additive: a decay emits at most one β particle (none in EC, IT or α decay unless you give a β⁺ share), any number of γ rays (none for pure β emitters or nuclides without listed lines) — so decays/s is larger than β/s + γ/s only where such decays dominate, and the time curves differ because each nuclide contributes with a different weight. The plots can show the decays per second (activity, Bq), the γ emission rate (γ/s = decays/s × the sum of the absolute intensities of the discrete γ lines of the nuclide) or the number of atoms. The γ data are the discrete lines of the first spectrum record (STYP 0) of MF 8 / MT 457 of the uploaded ENDF-6 decay files: energy ER (eV) and intensity FD·RI per decay; the internal database has no γ data. X-rays, annihilation radiation, conversion electrons and continua are not read. With uploaded decay data the plot gets the button Select a time slice → γ spectrum (drag over a time range of the plot). A window opens with: the spectrum at 1 keV per channel of the total and of the individual isotopes (legend entries show/hide a curve; drag to zoom), the statistics inside the plot: slice and live time, γ emitted per second and in total, detected counts and counts per second, mean efficiency and mean decay rate. The slice limits can be edited (seconds since the start of the irradiation); the window also has the Y axis (logarithmic or linear), the Y units (counts per keV or per second and keV) and the number of isotopes drawn. The detector and the data acquisition are configured in their own window, opened with the button Configure the Ge detector and DAQ (below the irradiation text of the “Irradiation and activation” panel, and in the γ-spectrum window): a Gaussian energy resolution (FWHM in keV, constant or given at 1 MeV and scaled with √E), the detector efficiency (a constant, a curve “energy keV, efficiency” with log-log interpolation, or the Ge detector geometry model of section 11.9; each model shows its own input boxes) and the DAQ parameters (pulse width τ, pulses per full-energy count and the pile-up spectrum, section 11.8). The changes are applied at once to every γ spectrum and saved with the setup. The decays of every isotope in the slice are exact: Di = Ni(t1) − Ni(t2) + Ri·(time of production in the slice) + Σp bp→i Dp (parents first), the slice limits being added to the time grid. The table lists, per isotope, decays, γ emitted and detected, and the strongest lines; the plot, the spectrum (all isotopes in columns) and the table can be saved as CSV. Not modelled: coincidence summing, dead time and the full detector response (peak tails); the Compton continuum and escape peaks appear only in the simulated Ge spectrum of the Ge model (section 11.9).

Efficiency curve model. One line per energy: energy (keV), total efficiency, peak efficiency (separators: blank, comma or semicolon). A line with two values is energy, peak efficiency; the missing total efficiency is then taken from the Ge geometry model (never below the peak). If the box is empty, the default curve is listed in it: the values (energy, total, peak at 14 energies from 50 keV to 3 MeV) of the Ge geometry model with the parameters set under “Ge detector (geometry model)”, or your own default. Buttons: Load the default curve, Save this curve as my default (stored with the setup and used from then on instead of the Ge-model values), Fill from the Ge geometry model (recomputes the table after you change the geometry) and Forget my default. If you clear the box and do not reopen the window, the efficiency is computed directly from the Ge geometry model. Interpolation is log-log; outside the range the end values are kept.

The Configure the Ge detector and DAQ window also draws the efficiency plot (peak and total for the Ge geometry model; the typed curve for the curve model), with the same legend, Y-axis and zoom controls as in the γ-ray spectrum window.
